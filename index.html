B7-33
the relaxin remix

A simplified, single-chain copy of relaxin — the pregnancy hormone with heart-failure potential. An elegant piece of protein engineering with animal data only, no human trials, and no approved use.

Quick read

B7-33 is an engineered single-chain analog of relaxin, designed to capture the hormone’s protective signals — anti-fibrotic, vessel-relaxing — in a simpler molecule. Its famous relative, serelaxin (recombinant relaxin-2), reached phase 3 trials for acute heart failure with mixed results. B7-33 itself has no human trials; the evidence is animal studies. It is not a medicine, not approved anywhere. Scroll for the full picture.

02Relaxin story

A century from childbirth hormone to heart-failure hope — then a remix for the next attempt.

Relaxin was discovered for its role in childbirth — loosening ligaments and vessels. For decades, that was the whole story.

A second act in the heart

Researchers found relaxin receptors in the heart and vessels, and a new idea formed: a natural hormone that fights fibrosis and relaxes vessels could help failing hearts.

Serelaxin’s mixed finale

B7-33 vs Relaxin-2 — the full comparison

🧬 Structure

Relaxin-2: two chains linked by disulfide bridges — elegant, complex to synthesize. B7-33: a single B-chain-derived stretch — engineered for simplicity.

🎯 RXFP1 interaction

Relaxin: the native key for RXFP1 — full, natural engagement. B7-33: designed to fit the same lock; experiments show it engages the receptor with preferential pERK signaling.

📡 Signaling bias

Relaxin triggers the full downstream program. B7-33 appears biased toward pERK in relevant cells — a narrower signal, which could be a feature or a limitation.

⏳ Stability

A simpler single chain is easier to build and handle than a cross-linked two-chain hormone — that is the engineering bet. Human pharmacology: untested.

🏁 Research status

Serelaxin: phase 3, mixed, unapproved. B7-33: animals only — it inherits the hypothesis, not the results.

⚖️ The verdict

Elegance is not evidence. B7-33 is a cleverer key; whether it opens anything in people is unknown.

The scienceAn engineered single-chain analog of relaxin-2: one continuous peptide chain designed to activate the RXFP1 receptor, unlike natural relaxin-2, which is built from two chains held together by disulfide bonds. It was developed as a simplified, easier-to-produce version of relaxin-2 that preferentially drives the pERK signaling branch downstream of RXFP1. In animal studies it has been reported to produce anti-fibrotic effects — reduced scar-tissue buildup — in models relevant to heart disease. All published evidence for B7-33 comes from preclinical (cell and animal) work; it has never been tested in humans and is not an approved medicine anywhere.

Plain EnglishB7-33 is a lab-built, simplified copy of the natural hormone relaxin-2. The original hormone is made of two chains; this copy squeezes the working part into a single chain, which is easier to manufacture. It is designed to switch on one specific receptor (RXFP1), favoring one particular downstream signal called pERK. In animal studies it appeared to reduce scarring in organs — but no human has ever been studied with it, so none of that is proven benefit in people.

The scienceA peptide hormone first identified in 1926, originally known for loosening ligaments and connective tissue during pregnancy. Natural human relaxin-2 is a two-chain molecule — an A-chain and a B-chain linked by disulfide bonds — and it signals mainly through the cell-surface receptor RXFP1. Beyond pregnancy, relaxin has been studied for cardiovascular effects, including vasodilation, and for anti-fibrotic effects, meaning it may counter the buildup of fibrous scar tissue. It is the natural template that B7-33 was engineered to copy.

Plain EnglishRelaxin is a natural hormone discovered in 1926, first known for its role in pregnancy. Scientists later found it could also widen blood vessels and seemed to resist scar-tissue buildup, which made it interesting for heart research. B7-33 is a simplified lab version of this hormone — relaxin is the original it is modeled on.

The scienceRelaxin family peptide receptor 1 — a cell-surface receptor belonging to the G-protein-coupled receptor family. RXFP1 is found in tissues including the heart, blood vessels, kidneys, and reproductive organs, which explains relaxin's wide range of studied effects. Both natural relaxin and B7-33 bind to this receptor; B7-33 was designed to engage it with a simpler, single-chain structure.

Plain EnglishRXFP1 is a receptor — a kind of sensor sitting on the surface of cells — that relaxin plugs into. When something binds to it, it switches on chemical signals inside the cell, including pathways known as cAMP and pERK. It is found in the heart, blood vessels, kidneys, and reproductive organs, which is why relaxin affects so many body systems. Both the natural hormone and B7-33 target this same receptor; B7-33 is just a simpler key designed to fit the same lock.

Anti-fibrotic research aims to slow, prevent, or reverse this scarring process. Reducing fibrosis in the heart is one of the main hoped-for effects of relaxin-based research, including the animal studies of B7-33.

Plain EnglishFibrosis is the medical word for internal scarring — tough, fibrous tissue building up inside an organ after injury or long-term disease.
